How it works

From a box of albums to a sale, in four steps

Most album stamps are common. The work is finding the few that are not, and selling those well.

  1. Photograph the page

    One photo of an album or stockbook page. The app finds and crops every stamp on it.

  2. Sort it

    A quick pass flags the stamps worth a closer look, and spots the ones you already own.

  3. Identify the good ones

    A full look gives catalogue numbers, condition, measured centering, and a value with its sources.

  4. Plan the sale

    See what you would keep from eBay, HipStamp, Delcampe, an auction house, a dealer or a group lot.

The camera

It waits for a sharp photo, then takes it for you

Hold the phone over the stamp, the way a banking app photographs a cheque. When the stamp is square, in focus and free of glare, the photo is taken. It keeps the sharpest frame, straightens it, and measures the centering.

Page sort

One photo of a page, every stamp on it sorted

Each stamp gets a quick identification and a value band. The few worth a closer look are marked, with the reason. Identify those on their own, and save the rest as a list.

Values

Every value says what it rests on

An estimate names its sources, such as a catalogue, auction records or general knowledge. Add the prices similar stamps really sold for, and the planning price moves to their median. The evidence is rated Strong, Fair or Rough.

Canadian rules built in

Made for Canadian and British North America stamps

It identifies stamps from any country. Canadian collectors also get the details other apps leave out.

  • Unitrade numbersListed beside Scott and Stanley Gibbons, each marked likely or unsure.
  • Where it was printedThe 1851 pence issue and the 1859 decimals were printed in New York. Origin can matter at the border, so it is recorded.
  • U.S. duty and customsCanadian-origin stamps sent to U.S. buyers face a 50% duty since August 22, 2026, and formal customs entry from October 22. The planner warns you.
  • Canada Post and the Greene FoundationPostage by value and destination, and valuable BNA stamps pointed to the Greene Foundation for a certificate.

Questions

Before you start

How accurate is it?

It is often right on common stamps and says so when it is unsure. Each result names what to check by hand, such as the perforation or watermark. Many stamps differ only by those details. For a valuable stamp, get an expert certificate before you sell.

Do I need an account?

Not to try it. Use it inside Claude with your own Claude account, or in your browser with your own Claude API key. Paid plans will add a sign-in, so you can use it without a key.

What does a credit buy?

One standard identification, or a sort of up to 30 stamps from one page photo. An expert identification uses two credits. Listing drafts come free with each identified stamp.

Where do my photos and records go?

Photos stay on your device until you ask for an identification. Then that photo goes to Anthropic's Claude to be read. Your collection, notes and purchase details stay on your device.

Does it handle blocks, covers and postal history?

Yes. Choose Block or Cover before you take the photo. A cover is valued as a whole item, including its postmarks and route.

Can I sell through the app?

Not directly. It writes the listing and works out where you would keep the most, and you post the listing yourself.

Is it only for Canadian stamps?

No. It identifies stamps from any country. It knows Canadian catalogues, postage and customs especially well.
